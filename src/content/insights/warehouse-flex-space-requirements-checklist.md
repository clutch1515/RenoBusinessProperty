Industrial and flex buildings succeed or fail on operational details. I ask clients to write down how their operation works before we tour anything. It makes the search faster and the options better.

Northern Nevada’s industrial market spans small flex units in Reno and Sparks, larger distribution buildings in areas such as the North Valleys and along I-80 east of Sparks, and parks in neighboring Storey County. The right building depends less on headline square footage and more on how product, vehicles, and people move through the operation.

## Start with the operation, not the building

- What is received, stored, assembled, serviced, or shipped?
- How many inbound and outbound deliveries happen per day, and in what vehicle types?
- How many employees work per shift, and how many visitors or customers arrive?
- What equipment is used, and what are its power, ventilation, and floor-load needs?
- What growth is expected over the lease term?

## Loading and truck access

- Number of dock-high doors and grade-level (drive-in) doors needed
- Truck court depth and turning room for the largest vehicles used
- Trailer parking or staging requirements
- Separation between truck traffic and employee or customer parking
- Access to I-80, I-580/US-395, and the routes your drivers use daily

## Clear height and layout

Clear height is the usable height below the lowest obstruction, such as beams, sprinkler lines, or lights. It determines how high product can be racked. Column spacing, bay depth, and the shape of the floor plate affect aisle layout and efficiency. Ask for verified specifications and confirm them on site.

## Power and utilities

- Electrical service size, voltage, and phase
- Natural gas availability
- Water, sewer, and any process or wash-down needs
- Heating, cooling, and ventilation for people and equipment
- Data and telecommunications providers serving the building

Upgrading power can be slow and costly, so confirm capacity early when equipment is power-intensive.

## Fire protection and storage

The building’s sprinkler system and fire code requirements can limit what is stored, how high, and in what quantity. High-piled storage, certain plastics, and hazardous materials may require additional review or upgrades. A fire protection professional can confirm whether the system supports the intended storage.

## Yard and outdoor use

Fenced storage, trailer parking, and outdoor work areas are valuable and not always permitted. Confirm what zoning, the lease, and any recorded restrictions allow, as well as drainage, paving condition, and security.

## Office ratio, parking, and people

[Flex users](/flex-warehouse-space/) often need more office, showroom, or restroom capacity than a pure warehouse provides. Estimate employee and customer parking needs per shift, accessibility requirements, and whether the existing office layout works or must be rebuilt.

## Verify the use and the jurisdiction

Industrial parks around Reno can sit in different cities and counties, each with its own zoning and permitting process. Confirm that the use is allowed and which approvals are needed before committing. See [Before You Lease: Verify That Your Business Use Fits the Location](/insights/verify-business-use-zoning-reno/).

## Compare total cost and flexibility

Add base rent, operating expenses, improvements, utilities, maintenance responsibilities, and moving downtime. Then weigh expansion rights, term length, and the cost of a building that limits the operation. [How to Compare Commercial Spaces Beyond the Asking Rent](/insights/compare-commercial-spaces-beyond-asking-rent/) provides a framework.

## How I approach it

On tours, I bring the written requirement and check each building against it: door counts, clear height, power, parking, and yard. A building that misses a must-have comes off the list, no matter how good the rate looks.

## Common questions

### What is the difference between flex and warehouse space?

Flex space typically combines a higher share of office or showroom area with some warehouse or service space, often with grade-level doors. Warehouse and distribution buildings prioritize storage volume, clear height, and truck loading.

### Are listing specifications reliable?

They are a useful starting point but should be verified. Clear height, power, sprinkler design, and door counts can change with renovations or be summarized imprecisely.

### How early should I start an industrial search?

Earlier than most people expect, especially when the operation needs specific power, loading, or yard features, or when improvements and permits are required before move-in.

### Practical takeaway

Write down how the operation works, translate it into building requirements, and verify them on site before you compare rents.
