When an owner asks me what a property is worth, I start with a few questions and a short list of documents. Good information leads to a faster, more reliable opinion, and better decisions.

Owners ask about value for many reasons: a possible sale, a refinance, a partnership change, estate planning, a lease renewal, or simply to understand where the property stands. The purpose matters, because it shapes which information is most important and what type of analysis is appropriate. [Commercial Property Values](/commercial-property-values/) explains the approaches used.

## Clarify the purpose and timing

- Are you considering a sale, refinance, lease-up, improvement, or long-term hold?
- Is there a deadline, such as a loan maturity, lease expiration, or tax event?
- Does a lender, court, or tax authority require a formal appraisal?

A broker’s opinion of value can help with strategy and pricing discussions. When a lender, court, or tax matter requires a formal appraisal, that work should come from a licensed appraiser.

## Gather income and lease information

- Current rent roll with tenant names, suite sizes, rents, and lease dates
- Copies of leases, amendments, and renewal or expansion options
- Tenant payment history and any outstanding defaults
- Recent operating-expense reconciliations

For income-producing property, lease terms often drive value as much as the building itself. Remaining term, scheduled increases, options, and who pays which expenses all matter.

## Gather operating statements and taxes

- Two or three years of income and expense statements, plus the current year to date
- Property tax bills and any pending assessments
- Insurance premiums and coverage summaries
- Utility, maintenance, and service contracts

## Document the physical property

- Building and land size, site plan, and floor plans if available
- Year built and the dates of major renovations
- Roof, HVAC, paving, and other system ages and condition
- Known deferred maintenance and planned capital work
- Any environmental, survey, or property condition reports

## Note the legal and site details

Zoning, permitted uses, parking, access, easements, and any recorded restrictions can expand or limit what a buyer or tenant can do with the property. Note anything unusual so it can be considered rather than discovered later.

## Think about the market evidence

Useful evidence includes verified sales of similar properties, current leasing activity, and competing availability. Not all published information is reliable or complete, and restricted data sources cannot always be shared. A good review explains which evidence was used and why it is comparable.

## What to expect from the conversation

A value discussion should explain the reasoning, not just a number. Expect to talk about the range of likely outcomes, the assumptions behind them, and the steps that could improve the result, such as resolving a vacancy, renewing a tenant, or completing deferred work. For broader decision support, see the [Property Owners](/property-owners/) page.

## How I approach it

You do not need every document before we talk. I usually start with the rent roll, the leases, and recent operating statements, then identify what else would change the answer.

## Common questions

### Is an online value estimate useful for commercial property?

It can be a rough starting point, but automated estimates rarely account for leases, income, condition, and use, which often drive commercial value.

### Do I need every document before talking to someone?

No. Start with what you have. Part of the first conversation is identifying what information matters most for your specific decision.

### How does a pending lease renewal affect value?

A renewal can change the income, term, and risk a buyer or lender sees. It is often worth evaluating the renewal and the value question together. See [Before Renewing a Commercial Tenant](/insights/commercial-tenant-renewal-questions-property-owners/).

### Practical takeaway

Define the purpose, gather what you already have, and expect an explanation of the reasoning, not just a number.
