Two sizable projects are taking shape in north Sparks, and I think their combined effect is worth watching. Renown Health is building a new outpatient care center in the Kiley Ranch area, while Barclay Group is developing Kiley Ranch Marketplace along Pyramid Way. One brings healthcare services closer to growing neighborhoods. The other adds a major new shopping destination. For [nearby retail property owners](/retail-storefronts/), the interesting question is what happens when both start bringing more activity to the same corridor.

## What's being built near Kiley Ranch?

Renown [broke ground in September 2026](https://www.renown.org/about/newsroom/renown-health-breaks-ground-on-new-ambulatory-care-center-in-sparks) on a **$140 million, 123,000-square-foot ambulatory care center** near Pyramid Way, Sparks Boulevard and Highland Ranch Parkway. Plans call for 134 exam rooms, primary and urgent care, pediatrics, women's health, imaging, laboratory, pharmacy and other outpatient services. It is **not a children's hospital**, although pediatric care will be available.

Renown expects initial services to begin in December 2027, and [Northern Nevada Business Weekly reported November 3](https://www.nnbw.com/news/2026/nov/03/bring-healthcare-to-where-people-live/) that the main building is expected to open in the first quarter of 2028. Those are projected schedules, not completed openings.

Nearby, [Barclay Group's Kiley Ranch Marketplace](https://barclaygroup.com/project/kiley-ranch/) is planned for approximately **413,600 square feet on 46.7 acres** at Pyramid Way and Wingfield Hills Road. The development plan includes large-format retail, smaller shop buildings and restaurant pads. [Smith's has announced a roughly 123,000-square-foot Marketplace store](https://www.nnbw.com/news/2026/aug/11/smiths-to-build-new-store-in-sparks/), including a pharmacy and fuel center, with an anticipated fall 2027 opening. Other planned spaces and tenant concepts may change as the project progresses.

## More activity does not automatically mean higher rents

For a small retail center on Pyramid Way, a new grocery anchor and a nearby medical destination could introduce more reasons for people to travel through the area. Patients, employees, shoppers and neighboring households may support demand for coffee, food, personal services and other everyday uses. But that is a possible outcome, not a measured increase in customer traffic or sales.

The other side is competition. New construction can offer tenants modern storefronts, updated systems, newer parking areas, and purpose-built restaurant pads. An established strip center may not be competing on the same terms, particularly if an incoming tenant needs significant improvements or a drive-through configuration.

And higher corridor traffic does not help every property equally. A center can sit near a busy intersection yet still have difficult access, limited visibility, or a tenant mix that does not capture those trips. The details matter more than the development headline.

## Three questions existing retail owners should ask

1. **Who is my center serving today?** A neighborhood service tenant may benefit from the area's growth without competing directly against a new national chain. An overlapping retail use may face more pressure.
2. **How does my property's access compare?** Look at turning movements, signage, parking, visibility and how easily customers can enter and leave during the times they actually shop.
3. **What would it cost to attract the next tenant?** Compare achievable rent against downtime, tenant improvements and leasing costs. A newer building may command a higher asking rent, but a well-positioned second-generation space can offer a different value proposition.

Owners with an upcoming [lease renewal](/insights/commercial-tenant-renewal-questions-property-owners/) may want to review how their existing tenants fit into the corridor's changing tenant mix. Businesses considering a new location should also compare [site access, visibility and the full occupancy cost](/insights/retail-site-selection-checklist-reno-sparks/), rather than relying on the appeal of a new development alone.

## The opportunity is in the details

I see the Kiley Ranch projects as an important development story for north Sparks and Spanish Springs. They may strengthen the corridor's position as a place to shop and obtain services, while also changing the competitive landscape for nearby retail centers. Both can be true.

There is still a lot we cannot conclude from project announcements. We do not yet know the resulting customer patterns, which planned tenant spaces will open on what dates, or how rents at any particular existing property will change. Those questions require property-level analysis as the area develops.
