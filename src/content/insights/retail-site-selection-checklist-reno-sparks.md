A [retail location](/retail-storefronts/) has to work at street level. Before I tour storefronts in Reno or Sparks with a client, we work through a short list of questions so every visit is productive.

Visibility and rent matter, but customers also need to find the business, get into the lot, park, and reach the front door easily, and the business needs the right infrastructure, signage rights, and neighbors to succeed. This is the checklist I use as a first pass.

## 1. Define the customer and the trade area

- Who is the customer, and how far will they travel for this business?
- Is the business a destination, or does it depend on convenience and impulse visits?
- Which neighborhoods, employment areas, or traffic corridors does the business need to reach?
- Where are the closest competitors and complementary businesses?

Reno and Sparks trade areas are shaped by freeways, river crossings, and a handful of major arterials, so two sites a short distance apart can draw very different customers.

## 2. Test access and arrival

- Which side of the street serves the customer’s typical trip, morning or evening?
- Can drivers turn in easily, or are there medians and restricted turns?
- How many entrances and curb cuts serve the center, and are they shared?
- Where do deliveries arrive, and do they conflict with customer parking?

## 3. Check visibility and signage

Visibility from the road is only valuable if the business can identify itself. Confirm building signage size and placement, monument or pylon sign availability, sight lines from approaching traffic, and what both the local sign code and the lease actually allow.

## 4. Observe parking during your peak hours

Total parking spaces matter less than how parking performs when your customers arrive. Visit at the times that matter most for the business, and ask whether neighboring uses share the same peak. A restaurant next to a fitness studio may compete for the same evening spaces.

## 5. Evaluate co-tenancy and neighbors

- Do neighboring businesses attract the same customers?
- Is there an anchor tenant, and how long is its lease expected to run?
- Does the center have vacancies, and how is it maintained?
- Do existing leases include exclusive-use clauses that could restrict your business?

## 6. Confirm the use and infrastructure

Zoning, utility capacity, ventilation, grease interceptors, restroom counts, and accessibility can all decide whether a concept works in a particular space. Food, beverage, medical, and personal-service uses usually need the most investigation. See [Before You Lease: Verify That Your Business Use Fits the Location](/insights/verify-business-use-zoning-reno/) for a step-by-step approach.

## 7. Estimate full occupancy cost

Add base rent, common-area charges, taxes, insurance, improvements, utilities, maintenance, and moving costs. Retail centers often have meaningful common-area expenses, so ask for recent operating cost history. [What Does NNN Mean in a Commercial Lease?](/insights/what-does-nnn-mean-commercial-lease/) explains what to look for.

## 8. Read the lease provisions that matter most to retail

- Permitted use and any exclusive rights
- Co-tenancy protections tied to an anchor or occupancy level
- Operating hours and continuous-operation requirements
- Signage approvals and responsibilities
- Relocation rights the landlord may hold
- Radius restrictions on opening another location nearby

## How I approach it

I like to see finalist sites at the hours that matter most to the business. A short stop at the lunch or evening peak often answers questions a listing never will.

## Common questions

### Is a corner location always better?

Not always. Corners can offer strong visibility, but access, turning movements, and parking may be harder. Judge the location by how easily customers can arrive and park.

### How important are traffic counts?

They are one useful input, but they do not show which direction drivers are heading, whether they can turn in, or whether they are likely customers. Combine counts with on-site observation.

### Should owners use this checklist too?

Yes. Retail property owners can use the same questions to understand how prospective tenants evaluate their property and which improvements may help leasing. More owner guidance is on the [Retail & Storefronts](/retail-storefronts/) page.

### Practical takeaway

Visit at the hours that matter, verify the use early, and compare locations by total cost and customer access, not rent and visibility alone.
