When an owner asks me “should I sell?”, I treat it as two questions: what could the property bring today, and what is it likely to produce if you keep it? A good decision puts both answers on the same timeline.

Owners in Reno, Sparks, and across Northern Nevada usually face this choice at a turning point: a lease expiration, a loan maturity, a partnership change, a large capital project, or simply a sense that it may be time. The framework below helps separate the financial comparison from the personal one, so each gets the attention it deserves.

## Start with your goals, not the market

Before looking at prices, write down what the property is supposed to do for you. Some owners want steady income with little involvement. Others want to free up capital for another investment, simplify an estate, or reduce exposure to a single building. The right answer for one owner can be the wrong answer for another with the same property.

- What is your time horizon for this property?
- How much involvement in management do you want?
- Do you need the capital for something else?
- Are partners, heirs, or lenders part of the decision?

## Estimate the “sell” path

A realistic sale estimate starts with a supported opinion of value, then subtracts the costs of getting there. [Preparing for a Commercial Property Value Review](/insights/prepare-commercial-property-value-review/) explains what information helps.

- Likely sale price range, based on income, condition, and comparable evidence
- Commissions, closing costs, and any loan payoff or prepayment terms
- Time on market and any concessions a buyer may ask for
- Tax consequences, which should be reviewed with a qualified tax adviser

## Estimate the “hold” path

Holding is not free. Project the income and costs over a realistic period, such as the next five to ten years, and include the events you can already see coming.

- Expected rent, including renewals, increases, and possible vacancy
- Operating expenses that are not passed through to tenants
- Capital work such as roofs, HVAC, paving, and tenant improvements
- Leasing commissions and downtime when space turns over
- Financing terms, including any upcoming maturity
- A reasonable estimate of what the property could sell for at the end

## Compare on the same basis

Once both paths are estimated, compare them over the same period. Ask what the sale proceeds could reasonably earn if reinvested, and compare that with the income and eventual sale value from holding. Small differences in assumptions can change the answer, so test a few scenarios rather than relying on one projection.

## Weigh the risks you cannot put in a spreadsheet

- **Tenant concentration:** a single-tenant building carries more vacancy risk than a multi-tenant property.
- **Capital surprises:** older systems may need replacement sooner than planned.
- **Market timing:** nobody reliably predicts the market; plan for a range of outcomes.
- **Your time:** the effort of ownership has real value, especially for self-managed properties.

## Options between selling and holding

The choice is not always all or nothing. Owners sometimes refinance to free up capital, renew a key tenant before selling to strengthen value, complete deferred work first, or bring in a partner. Each option changes the risk and timing, and each deserves the same side-by-side comparison.

## How I approach it

When I run this comparison for an owner, I test a few scenarios rather than one projection. Seeing how sensitive the answer is to rent, vacancy, and capital costs is often more useful than any single number.

## Common questions

### Is it better to sell with a tenant in place or vacant?

It depends on the buyer pool. Investors usually value a well-leased property with a strong tenant; owner-users may prefer a vacant building they can occupy. Knowing which buyers fit your property helps decide whether to renew, re-lease, or sell vacant.

### How long does a commercial sale usually take?

Timelines vary with property type, pricing, financing, and due diligence. Plan for marketing, negotiation, and a due diligence period, and allow extra time for properties with complex leases or physical issues.

### Should I renew a tenant before selling?

Often a renewal strengthens value for investor buyers, but the terms matter. See [Before Renewing a Commercial Tenant](/insights/commercial-tenant-renewal-questions-property-owners/) for the questions to ask.

### Practical takeaway

Estimate both paths honestly, compare them over the same period, and let your goals break the tie.
